04 · Questions

Questions, answered.

Before you open the workbench.

What does it compare?

Three cooling designs for the same site and workload: evaporative towers, air-cooled chillers and hybrid adiabatic coolers. For each one you get energy, PUE, water, carbon and operating cost, and what the design asks of the local water supply and power grid.

Why two ledgers?

The facility ledger is what the operator spends. The community ledger is what the shared water and power systems absorb: water headroom, groundwater stress and substation peak. The same choice moves both, and seeing them together shows who carries what.

Why is there no single score?

A combined score would hide the trade-off. Water, power and carbon stay side by side, and when no design meets every limit you set, the planner says so and names the limit that binds.

Can I test a what-if?

Yes. Change the site, season, workload, IT load or grid carbon and every ledger recalculates. Set facility and community limits and the planner returns the designs that meet all of them.

How are the figures checked?

Every number comes from one calculation engine. A separate verifier traces each figure in a brief back to that engine and withholds anything that does not match.

Who is it for?

Operators and developers choosing a cooling design, and the municipalities, utilities and community groups who want a transparent read of the same assessment. Reviewers can also check an applicant's stated figures against the modelled range.

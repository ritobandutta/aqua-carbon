01 · Engine

A model explains. The engine counts.

Four parts, each with one job. Every number comes from one deterministic engine, and every claim built on those numbers is checked by something that did not write it.

01

One engine writes every number

Seasonal physics and stated coefficients give energy, water, carbon and cost. A language model never types a figure.

02

A second process checks the first

A separate verifier traces each number in a brief back to the engine. A figure that matches nothing blocks the output.

03

Burdens stay apart

Water, power, carbon and cost remain separate lines. There is no combined score to hide the trade-off.

04

Coverage flags who the data misses

Satellite settlement footprints are compared with piped-water records to show where people may be uncounted.

04 · Questions

Straight answers.

What the tool claims, and what it does not.

Are these numbers real?

They are modelled. The engine runs on illustrative facility inputs and on regional values that are not yet verified against official files, and every figure is labelled that way. Read results as questions to ask, not findings.

Does AI write the figures?

No. A model can draft prose and read claims out of text, but every number comes from the engine. A separate verifier recomputes the figures and withholds anything that does not match. It checks that numbers are real, not that the wording is fair.

Why is there no single score?

A combined score would hide the trade-off the tool exists to show. Water, power and carbon stay side by side, and when no design meets every limit, the planner says so and names the binding constraint.

Who is it for?

Operators and developers choosing a cooling design, and the municipalities, utilities and community groups who want a transparent read of the same assessment.

What does it not do?

It does not control equipment, certify a design or claim verified savings. Results are modelled potential impact, and any real facility needs qualified engineering review.
